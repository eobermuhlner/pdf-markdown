# Clinical Encounter Note

**Chief Complaint:**  
- 48‑year‑old male reports intermittent chest pain radiating to the left arm, lasting 5–10 minutes, relieved by rest.

**History of Present Illness (HPI):**  
1. Onset: 3 weeks ago, gradual increase in frequency.  
2. Associated symptoms: shortness of breath, diaphoresis, nausea.  
3. Exacerbating factors: exertion; alleviated with sublingual nitroglycerin.

**Past Medical History:**  
- Hypertension (HTN) diagnosed 5 years ago, on lisinopril.  
- Hyperlipidemia managed with atorvastatin.  

**Physical Examination:**  
* Cardiovascular: S1‑S2 normal, no murmurs.  
* Pulmonary: clear to auscultation bilaterally.  
* Extremities: no cyanosis or edema.

```text
EKG Findings:
  ST‑segment depression in leads II, III, aVF.
  T wave inversions V5–V6.
```

**Assessment & Plan:**  
- **Acute coronary syndrome (unstable angina).**  
- Immediate cardiology consult.  
- Initiate dual antiplatelet therapy: aspirin 81 mg daily and clopidogrel 75 mg daily.  
- Continue beta‑blocker and statin.  
- Schedule cardiac catheterization within 24 hours.  

---  

## Follow‑Up & Education  
- Patient advised to avoid strenuous activity until cleared by cardiology.  
- Discussed lifestyle modifications: diet, exercise, smoking cessation.  
- Reviewed medication adherence and side effect monitoring.
